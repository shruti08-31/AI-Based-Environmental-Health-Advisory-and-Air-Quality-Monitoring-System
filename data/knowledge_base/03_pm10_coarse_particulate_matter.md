# Coarse Particulate Matter ($PM_{10}$) & Dust Resuspension
Source: WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines & CPCB Particulate Reference

$PM_{10}$ encompasses inhalable particles with aerodynamic diameters of 10 micrometers or smaller, including both fine particles and coarser thoracic fractions (2.5 to 10 micrometers). Coarse $PM_{10}$ is commonly associated with mechanical crushing, construction and demolition activities, unpaved roads, windblown soil dust, and resuspended road dust. Elevated $PM_{10}$ irritates the eyes, nose, and upper respiratory tract and aggravates chronic bronchitis and asthma.

---

## 1. Size Fractions & Physical Definitions
* **Total $PM_{10}$ (Thoracic Fraction):** All airborne particulate matter with an aerodynamic diameter $\le 10\,\mu\text{m}$ capable of passing through the larynx and entering the thoracic airways. By mass, ambient $PM_{10}$ typically contains $50\%\text{–}80\%$ fine particulate matter ($PM_{2.5}$) alongside the coarse fraction.
* **Coarse Particulate Fraction ($PM_{2.5\text{–}10}$ or $PM_{\text{coarse}}$):** Particles specifically between $2.5\,\mu\text{m}$ and $10\,\mu\text{m}$ in aerodynamic diameter. Unlike $PM_{2.5}$, which forms primarily from combustion and atmospheric chemical reactions, $PM_{2.5\text{–}10}$ is generated almost exclusively by mechanical fragmentation, abrasion, and wind-driven suspension.
* **$PM_{2.5}/PM_{10}$ Diagnostic Ratio:** Monitoring networks use the mass ratio of $PM_{2.5}$ to $PM_{10}$ to diagnose pollution sources in real time:
  * **High Ratio ($> 0.60$):** Indicates dominance of combustion exhaust, biomass burning, or secondary urban/industrial haze.
  * **Low Ratio ($< 0.40$):** Signals dominance of coarse crustal dust, construction debris, unpaved road dust resuspension, or regional dust storms.

---

## 2. Primary Sources & Dust Resuspension Mechanisms
Coarse particulate emissions originate from both anthropogenic mechanical disturbances and natural windblown processes:
* **Vehicular Road Dust Resuspension:** Moving vehicles generate aerodynamic tire-wake turbulence and mechanical shear stress that lift settled silt, brake-pad wear, tire-tread rubber, and crustal minerals back into the air. Emissions scale strongly with vehicle weight (heavy-duty trucks), vehicle speed, and road surface silt loading.
* **Construction, Demolition & Mining:** Earthmoving, excavation, stone crushing, cement mixing, uncovered material haulage, and demolition release large plumes of alkaline mineral dust (calcium carbonate, silica, iron oxides).
* **Unpaved Roads & Arid Soil Erosion:** In semi-arid and dry-season conditions, low soil moisture and sparse vegetation allow surface winds exceeding threshold friction velocities to entrain loose topsoil and agricultural dust.
* **Natural & Biological Aerosols:** Sea-salt spray in coastal airsheds, plant pollen grains, fungal spores, and long-range desert dust storms.

---

## 3. Meteorological & Seasonal Dynamics
* **Dry Winds & Convective Mixing:** High wind speeds in dry weather increase horizontal shear stress on roads and bare ground, spiking coarse $PM_{10}$ even when local combustion emissions remain constant.
* **Precipitation Washout & Soil Moisture:** Rainfall scavenges airborne $PM_{10}$ through **wet deposition** and binds surface dust particles via capillary moisture bridges, suppressing road-dust resuspension until surfaces dry out.
* **Gravitational Settling:** Because coarse particles ($2.5\text{–}10\,\mu\text{m}$) have significantly higher terminal settling velocities than $PM_{2.5}$, they fall out of the atmosphere within **minutes to a few hours**, resulting in sharp localized concentration gradients near construction sites and busy road corridors.

---

## 4. Regulatory Standards & CPCB Breakpoints for $PM_{10}$
Because $PM_{10}$ includes $PM_{2.5}$, the WHO 2021 Global Air Quality Guidelines advise that $PM_{2.5}$ guidelines should govern whenever both pollutants are monitored, while maintaining distinct benchmarks for $PM_{10}$ to control coarse dust exposure:

| Regulatory / Guideline Framework | Averaging Period | Concentration Limit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **WHO 2021 Air Quality Guideline (AQG)** | 24-hour | $45\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$ |
| **WHO 2021 Air Quality Guideline (AQG)** | Annual | $15\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$ |
| **India CPCB NAAQS (Industrial, Residential, Rural & Ecologically Sensitive)** | 24-hour | $100\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$ |
| **India CPCB NAAQS (Industrial, Residential, Rural & Ecologically Sensitive)** | Annual | $60\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$ |

### CPCB IND-AQI Breakpoints for $PM_{10}$ (24-Hour Average)
* **Good (0–50):** $0\text{–}50\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$
* **Satisfactory (51–100):** $51\text{–}100\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$
* **Moderate (101–200):** $101\text{–}250\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$
* **Poor (201–300):** $251\text{–}350\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$
* **Very Poor (301–400):** $351\text{–}430\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$
* **Severe (401–500):** $> 430\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$

## 5. Respiratory Deposition & Health Effects
* **Upper & Thoracic Airway Impaction:** Due to their larger inertial momentum, coarse particles ($2.5\text{–}10\,\mu\text{m}$) cannot easily follow sharp airflow turns in the respiratory tract. They deposit primarily via **inertial impaction** in the nasopharynx, larynx, trachea, and main bronchi rather than reaching the deep alveoli.
* **Mucociliary Clearance:** Deposited coarse particles are trapped by airway mucus and swept upward by ciliated epithelial cells (the **mucociliary escalator**) within hours to days, where they are cleared via coughing or swallowing.
* **Acute & Chronic Clinical Impacts:**
  * Mechanical and chemical irritation of the ocular surface (red, watery eyes), nasal mucosa (rhinitis, sneezing), and throat.
  * Induction of bronchoconstriction, coughing, wheezing, and increased sputum production in individuals with **asthma** and **chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)** / chronic bronchitis.
  * Elevated short-term respiratory hospital admissions and emergency visits during dust storms and high-wind resuspension episodes.
  * When coarse dust contains crystalline silica, heavy metals, or adsorbed biological endotoxins, repeated exposure drives chronic airway inflammation.

---

## 6. Dust Suppression & Exposure Mitigation
According to WHO Good Practice Statements for sand and dust storms and urban road dust management:
* **Source & Municipal Controls:** Mechanical vacuum street sweeping (rather than dry broom sweeping, which re-entrains dust), water misting/sprinkling using treated wastewater on haul roads and construction sites, windbreak barriers, covering open trucks carrying aggregate/soil, and paving road shoulders.
* **Personal Protection:** Well-fitted **N95/FFP2** or **N99** respirators capture $>95\%$ to $>99\%$ of both $PM_{10}$ and $PM_{2.5}$. Even standard multi-layer surgical masks provide substantial mechanical filtration against coarse particles ($>2.5\,\mu\text{m}$), though they remain ineffective against fine $PM_{2.5}$ and gases.
