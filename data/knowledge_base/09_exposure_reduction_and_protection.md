# Practical Exposure Reduction & Environmental Protection Actions
Source: US EPA Indoor Air & Public Exposure Reduction Guidelines

Effective personal exposure reduction strategies include shifting vigorous workouts indoors during Poor, Very Poor, or Severe AQI tiers; avoiding busy traffic corridors and open burning plumes; keeping windows closed during outdoor pollution spikes; operating portable HEPA air cleaners in occupied rooms; avoiding indoor smoke sources such as tobacco, incense, or unvented combustion; and wearing a well-fitted N95 or FFP2 particulate respirator when outdoor travel is unavoidable. Community-level protection relies on cleaner transport, dust suppression, and emission controls.

---

## 1. Personal & Behavioral Exposure Reduction
Because inhaled pollutant dose is the product of **ambient concentration $\times$ exposure duration $\times$ minute ventilation rate**, modifying daily routines substantially lowers internal lung burden even during regional pollution episodes:
* **Modify Exercise Intensity & Location:** Vigorous aerobic exercise (running, brisk cycling, competitive sports) increases breathing volume 5- to 10-fold and shifts breathing from the nose to the mouth, bypassing upper-airway filtration. During **Poor (201–300)**, **Very Poor (301–400)**, or **Severe (401–500)** AQI tiers, cancel outdoor workouts and switch to low-intensity indoor activities (such as stretching, mobility routines, or yoga) in a filtered room.
* **Time Outdoor Activities Diurnally:**
  * **For Particulate Matter ($PM_{2.5}$ / $PM_{10}$):** Concentrations typically peak during early morning and late evening thermal inversions and rush hours; midday hours often offer better vertical atmospheric dispersion.
  * **For Ground-Level Ozone ($O_3$):** Because $O_3$ is driven by sunlight, concentrations peak between **12:00 PM and 5:00 PM**; schedule necessary outdoor walks in the early morning or after sunset.
* **Route Selection & Micro-Environment Avoidance:**
  * Walk or cycle along quiet residential streets rather than congested arterial roads; stepping back even **50 to 100 meters** from heavy traffic cuts exposure to primary tailpipe $NO_2$, $CO$, and ultrafine soot by 30%–60%.
  * Avoid standing near idling vehicles, diesel generators, construction excavation sites, or open waste/biomass burning plumes.
  * When commuting by car in heavy traffic or dusty conditions, close windows and set the cabin HVAC system to **recirculate** mode (taking periodic fresh-air breaks on long drives to prevent indoor $CO_2$ buildup).

## 2. Respiratory Protection & Mask Filtration Performance
Selecting the right respiratory protection requires matching the filter media to the dominant pollutant type:

| Mask / Respirator Type | Particulate Filtration ($PM_{2.5}$ & $PM_{10}$) | Gaseous Pollutant Removal ($O_3$, $NO_2$, $SO_2$, $CO$) | Critical Fit & Usage Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **N95 / KN95 / FFP2 Respirator** | **High ($\ge 95\%$ at $0.3\,\mu\text{m}$)** via electrostatic + mechanical capture | **None** (gases pass freely through particulate media) | Requires a tight perimeter facial seal; facial hair, loose ear loops, or pinched nose bridges cause significant inward leakage. |
| **N99 / FFP3 Respirator** | **Very High ($\ge 99\%$ at $0.3\,\mu\text{m}$)** | **None** | Higher breathing resistance; best suited for unavoidable outdoor travel during Severe AQI episodes. |
| **Activated-Carbon Cartridge Respirator** | Depends on pre-filter (e.g., P100/N95 combo) | **Selective** (adsorbs $O_3$, $NO_2$, $SO_2$, and VOCs; **does NOT filter $CO$**) | Heavy occupational device; cartridges saturate over time and require scheduled replacement. |
| **3-Ply Surgical / Medical Mask** | **Low to Moderate** (~30%–60% for $PM_{2.5}$; better for coarse $PM_{10}$) | **None** | Loose side gaps allow unfiltered ambient air to bypass the filter medium during inhalation. |
| **Cloth / Cotton Mask** | **Very Low ($< 10\%\text{–}20\%$ for $PM_{2.5}$)** | **None** | Large pore sizes fail to trap fine combustion aerosols; not recommended for air pollution protection. |

## 3. Indoor Air Quality Management & Clean-Air Shelter
People spend roughly 80%–90% of their day indoors, making indoor air management the single most effective defense during multi-day pollution episodes:
* **Building Envelope Sealing:** Keep exterior windows and doors closed during outdoor pollution spikes, dust storms, or nearby agricultural/waste burning. Use weatherstripping or damp towels along leaky window frames and door thresholds, and open windows for cross-ventilation only when outdoor AQI drops into the **Good** or **Satisfactory** range.
* **Portable HEPA Air Cleaners:**
  * Operate portable air purifiers equipped with certified **True HEPA (H13 or H14)** filters (which capture $\ge 99.95\%$ to $99.995\%$ of particles at $0.3\,\mu\text{m}$) in bedrooms and primary living spaces.
  * Match the unit's **Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR)** to room volume (ideally providing at least **4 to 5 air changes per hour**), keep doors closed in the filtered room, and run the unit continuously on medium/auto speed rather than intermittently.
  * **Avoid Ozone-Generating Devices:** Do not use electrostatic precipitators, UV-C ionizers, or "ozone generators," as they emit harmful ground-level ozone ($O_3$) indoors.
* **Eliminate Indoor Combustion & Aerosol Sources:**
  * Strictly prohibit indoor tobacco/e-cigarette smoking, incense sticks (*agarbatti*), scented candles, mosquito coils, and unvented biomass/kerosene heating.
  * Use externally vented range hoods or exhaust fans whenever cooking—especially during high-temperature frying or gas-stove use, which generate substantial indoor $PM_{2.5}$ and $NO_2$.
  * Clean indoor floors with a **damp mop** or HEPA-filtered vacuum rather than dry sweeping or feather dusting, which resuspends settled $PM_{10}$ and $PM_{2.5}$.

## 4. Community, Urban & Industrial Emission Controls
Sustained improvements in regional air quality depend on systemic source-reduction policies alongside individual protection:
* **Clean Transportation & Mobility:** Transitioning public bus and commercial fleets to electric or CNG propulsion, enforcing strict tailpipe emission norms (e.g., BS-VI / Euro 6 standards), expanding public transit and non-motorized infrastructure, and phasing out older high-emitting diesel vehicles.
* **Construction & Road Dust Suppression:** Mandating windbreak enclosures and green netting around construction sites, covering bulk material transport trucks, deploying mechanical vacuum road sweepers, and misting haul roads with treated water.
* **Industrial & Energy Controls:** Enforcing continuous emission monitoring systems (CEMS), requiring flue-gas desulfurization (FGD) and electrostatic precipitators/baghouses on thermal power plants and industrial boilers, and shifting industrial clusters from coal/petcoke to natural gas or renewable power.
* **Waste & Agricultural Residue Management:** Enforcing bans on open municipal solid-waste burning, expanding decentralized composting/recycling, and promoting in-situ crop residue management (such as Happy Seeder machinery and bio-decomposers) to curb seasonal stubble-burning plumes.
