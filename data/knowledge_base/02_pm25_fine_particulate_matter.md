# Fine Particulate Matter (PM2.5) Characteristics & Health Impacts
Source: WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines & US EPA PM2.5 Reference

PM2.5 refers to airborne particles with aerodynamic diameters of 2.5 micrometers or smaller. Because of their microscopic size, PM2.5 particles bypass upper airway filtration, penetrate deep into pulmonary alveoli, and can enter systemic circulation. Primary sources include motor vehicle exhaust, fossil fuel combustion, industrial boilers, and biomass or crop residue burning, alongside secondary aerosols formed from SO2 and NO2. Short- and long-term exposure is linked to airway inflammation, asthma exacerbation, reduced lung function, and cardiovascular stress.
